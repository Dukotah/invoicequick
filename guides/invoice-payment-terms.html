Invoice Payment Terms Explained — Net 30, Net 15, Due on Receipt

Payment terms tell your client when the invoice is due and what happens if they pay late. Choosing the wrong terms — or wording them vaguely — is one of the most common reasons freelancers get paid slowly. This guide explains every standard term, when to use each, and the exact language to put on your invoice.

What invoice payment terms are — and why they matter

Invoice payment terms specify two things: when you expect to be paid, and what incentives or penalties apply to early or late payment. They are not just boilerplate — they set the client’s expectation from the moment the invoice lands in their inbox.

An invoice with no stated terms leaves the client to decide when “soon” means. An invoice that says “Due: 17 November 2026 — Net 14” is harder to ignore because it names a date. Most slow-payment situations are not malicious — they are administrative. Clear terms remove the ambiguity that allows an invoice to sit in a processing queue for six weeks.

The common payment terms, defined

Most common

Net 30

The full invoice amount is due within 30 calendar days of the invoice date. “Net” means the total — no discount applied. This is the standard for most B2B freelance and agency work, and it is what most corporate accounts payable departments are set up to process. It is the right default for established clients and project-based billing.

Freelance default

Net 14 & Net 15

Payment due within 14 or 15 calendar days of the invoice date. Net 14 is increasingly common as a freelance default because it halves the cash-flow gap compared to Net 30 while still giving most clients plenty of processing time. For one-off projects, smaller billings, and clients you have worked with before, Net 14 is a reasonable standard to set.

Short projects

Net 7

Payment due within 7 calendar days of the invoice date. Appropriate for small invoices, single-session work (photography, copywriting, same-day consulting), and clients who have agreed to fast payment. Avoid as a default for larger billings with corporate clients — their payment approval cycles often cannot move in seven days regardless of intent.

Immediate

Due on Receipt / Payable on Receipt

The invoice should be paid immediately upon receiving it — same day or within 24–48 hours. Appropriate for completed, fully-delivered work where you have a close relationship with the client, for final project invoices when a deposit has already been paid, or when you have negotiated faster terms after a previous late-payment problem. Rarely appropriate as a blanket default for new clients.

Enterprise

Net 45 / Net 60

Payment due within 45 or 60 calendar days. Standard in large-enterprise procurement contracts and government work. If a corporate client’s standard purchase-order terms say Net 60, their accounts payable system will not process your invoice sooner regardless of what your invoice states. Accepting Net 60 is a cash-flow decision — factor the delay into your project pricing.

Which payment terms to use for your work

The right terms depend on your client type, the project size, and your cash-flow tolerance.

Client type Recommended terms Rationale
Individual / small business, first project Net 14 with a 25–50% deposit before start Deposit confirms budget; shorter terms reduce risk with an unproven payer
Regular client, on-time payment history Net 14 to Net 30 Reward good payers with the term length you are both comfortable with
Agency or SMB project billing Net 30 Industry standard; matches most AP department processing cycles
Enterprise / large corporate Accept their PO terms (often Net 30–60) or negotiate to Net 30 Their AP system runs on fixed cycles; a shorter term on your invoice rarely changes when they pay
Milestone invoice (mid-project) Net 14 Shorter terms on interim invoices reduce total unpaid balance
Final invoice (fully delivered) Net 7 to Net 14 Work is already accepted; a short window is reasonable
One rule beats all others: a specific due date on the invoice (“Due: 17 November 2026”) gets paid faster than a term alone (“Net 14”). Use both. The term states your policy; the date tells accounts payable exactly when to schedule the transfer.

Exactly what to write on the invoice

Payment terms belong in the Notes or Terms field near the bottom of the invoice. Here are ready-to-use versions for InvoiceQuick’s Notes field:

Standard freelance (Net 14):

Payment due: [specific date] — Net 14 from invoice date.
Bank transfer: [account details or 'details on file'].
Late payment: 1.5% per month on overdue balances per our agreement.

Standard project / agency (Net 30):

Payment due: [specific date] — Net 30 from invoice date.
Bank transfer: [account details].
Late payment: 1.5% per month applies to balances unpaid after the due date.

Due on Receipt (for final, fully-delivered work):

Due on receipt. Bank transfer: [account details].
Thank you for your business.

Include the specific date even when you also state the term. Accounts payable departments schedule transfers by calendar date, not by calculating from the invoice date manually.

Early payment discounts (2/10 Net 30)

An early payment discount offers the client a small percentage off the invoice total if they pay by an earlier date. The standard notation is 2/10 Net 30: take 2% off if you pay within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 30 days.

Early payment discounts are more common in B2B trade than in freelance work. They can be useful when you have a large invoice with a slow-paying corporate client and would rather receive 98% quickly than 100% in 45 days. The key caveat: the discount reduces your revenue, so treat it as a cash-flow cost. On a £5,000 invoice, 2% is £100 — decide whether two fewer weeks of chasing is worth it.

To use it, add to your Notes field: 2% early payment discount if paid by [date 10 days after invoice]; otherwise full amount due [date 30 days after invoice].

Late payment clauses — adding teeth to your terms

A late payment clause states the interest or flat fee that applies when an invoice is not paid by the due date. The clause belongs in your freelance contract or statement of work before work begins — not as a surprise on the invoice. The invoice can then reference it: “Late payment: 1.5% per month on overdue balances per our agreement.”

Common rates:

  • 1.5% per month (18% annualised) — the most common freelance rate in the US and UK; roughly matches what clients would pay on a commercial credit facility
  • Statutory rate — in the UK, the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 sets a statutory rate of 8% over the Bank of England base rate for qualifying B2B transactions; EU Directive 2011/7/EU provides a similar reference rate in EU member states. These apply without a specific contractual clause for qualifying commercial debts.
  • Flat fee — some freelancers charge a flat £25 / $25 admin fee instead of percentage interest. Simpler to communicate, but less deterrent on large invoices.

In practice, the main effect of a visible late payment clause is prevention, not collection. Most clients pay on time because they can see the clause — they rarely test it.

When a client asks for longer payment terms

A corporate client’s procurement team may state their “standard terms” are Net 60 or Net 90. You can accept, negotiate, or decline — but resolve it before the project starts, not after the first invoice goes out.

  • Price for the longer window. If you accept Net 60, factor that cash-flow cost into your rate. A project that ties up your revenue for 60 days costs more than one paid in 14 days.
  • Negotiate for milestone payments. Even if the final invoice is Net 60, ask for a 25–50% deposit up front. A statement of work with milestone payments distributes revenue across the project rather than concentrating it at the end.
  • Get it in writing before you start. Verbal agreement on payment terms is not enough. Any negotiated terms go into the signed contract or SOW. See the freelance contract guide for clause language.
  • Know which terms are non-negotiable for you. If you rely on a 30-day cash cycle, a Net 60 client is a business decision. A project needs to be worth the working-capital cost. It is acceptable to decline or raise your rate to compensate.

Frequently asked questions

What does Net 30 mean on an invoice?

Net 30 means the full invoice amount (“net” = total, no discount) is due within 30 calendar days of the invoice date. It is the most common standard in B2B freelance and agency work and is what most corporate accounts payable departments are set up to process by default.

What is the difference between Net 30 and Due on Receipt?

Net 30 gives the client 30 days from the invoice date to pay. Due on Receipt means payment is expected immediately — the same day or within 24–48 hours. Due on Receipt is appropriate for completed, fully-delivered work with trusted clients; Net 30 is the standard for project billing.

Is Net 30 30 calendar days or 30 business days?

Calendar days, unless you specify otherwise. To avoid all ambiguity, write the actual due date on the invoice (“Due: 3 December 2026 — Net 30 from invoice date”) rather than relying on the client to calculate it. A named date removes any question about how weekends and public holidays are counted.

What does 2/10 Net 30 mean?

It is an early payment discount: take 2% off the invoice if you pay within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due within 30 days. More common in B2B trade than in freelance work. Only offer it if the cash-flow benefit of being paid 20 days sooner outweighs the 2% revenue reduction.

Should I use Net 14 or Net 30 as a freelancer?

Net 14 is a practical default for most freelance work. It cuts the cash-flow gap in half compared to Net 30 while still giving most clients reasonable processing time. Reserve Net 30 for established corporate clients whose procurement systems require it. For smaller billings and final invoices, Net 14 or Net 7 is entirely normal.

What is a late payment fee and how do I add it?

A late payment fee is a charge that applies when an invoice is not paid by the due date. The standard freelance rate is 1.5% per month on the overdue balance. The clause belongs in your freelance contract or SOW before work starts — the invoice references it: “Late payment: 1.5% per month per our agreement.” Without a prior written agreement, adding a fee unilaterally may not be enforceable if disputed.

Where on an invoice do I put the payment terms?

In the Notes or Terms field near the bottom of the invoice. Best practice: state the specific due date and the term together (“Due: 17 November 2026 — Net 14”), followed by your bank details and a brief reference to the late payment clause.
